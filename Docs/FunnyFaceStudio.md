# Funny Face Studio

Children see one expressive puppet, try copying it with a grown-up (or watch together), and identify its pretend emotion from three illustrated choices. The six expressions are Happy, Sad, Mad, Worried, Surprised, and Calm. Each appears once in a shuffled session. Replay and reentry avoid both the previous first face and the last face displayed.

The copying step uses physical descriptions of eyes, eyebrows, and mouth without revealing the emotion word. There is no camera or automated assessment of the child. The child or caregiver decides when to move to naming. Each answer has a matching face thumbnail, a readable emotion word, and a speaker button. Incorrect choices give a gentle visual clue without advancing. A correct choice reveals and speaks the emotion before an explicit next button.

All sixty narration variants are bundled as Ruth recordings, including every shuffled ordering of the three answer names. The shared speech channel prevents overlapping voices. Vector faces share a color and character design, changing only their facial features. Mad also replaces Cross in the shared feeling picker used by My Feeling Weather.

The activity identifies the intended expression of a pretend puppet. The caregiver tip preserves the distinction between face clues and knowing how a real person feels. [ZERO TO THREE recommends naming feelings and using pretend emotion charades together](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/your-calm-is-their-calm-co-regulation-strategies-for-infants-and-toddlers/).

Native validation covers 200 sessions, copy-before-identify sequencing, incorrect/stale/duplicate actions, nonrepeating faces, replay exclusions, distinct face renders, and exact Ruth coverage. UI validation plays all six rounds, exercises both copying choices, reads every answer aloud, tries incorrect and correct answers, and checks replay, reopening, and the Mad label in My Feeling Weather.
