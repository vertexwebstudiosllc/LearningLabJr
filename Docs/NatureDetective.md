# Nature Detective

Twenty-four shuffled mysteries replace the original three fixed rounds. Each target appears once per session. Replay and reentry avoid both the previous starting target and the last target displayed. Each round offers three stable, shuffled picture choices; distractors avoid all three choices from the preceding round.

Each animal has three progressively revealed clues. Children may answer at any point. A wrong guess reveals and narrates all three clues without advancing the round or removing choices. The shared speaker button repeats the current clue or feedback. A correct answer reveals a larger cutout, the name, and Ruth's celebration before the child moves on.

The bank includes twenty familiar farm, home, and ocean animals plus a butterfly, bee, ladybug, and ant. All images are transparent cutouts. The 121 catalog entries resolve to 120 unique Ruth recordings because two mysteries share their opening clue. These recordings are bundled for offline use.

Native tests cover 200 full sessions, nonrepeating targets, recent-choice exclusion, clue limits, wrong/unknown/stale/duplicate actions, early answers, exact Ruth coverage, and alpha transparency. The UI playthrough solves all 24 mysteries, exercises wrong answers and every clue, then checks replay and reentry.

## Educational sources

Clues use simple observable characteristics. Where species vary, scripts say "many" or explicitly describe the pictured animal. Insects are included as animals, and the star-shaped ocean animal is called a sea star.

- [Smithsonian: insects have six legs, three body segments, and one pair of antennae](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/conservation/news/house-hunters-amazing-arthropods)
- [Smithsonian: wasps, ants, and bees](https://www.si.edu/spotlight/buginfo/hymenoptera)
- [NOAA: sea stars and their tube feet](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/starfish.html)
